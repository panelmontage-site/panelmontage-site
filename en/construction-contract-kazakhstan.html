Construction Contracts and Work Process

A well-organized construction project starts with a clearly defined scope of work: what will be performed, which materials will be used, how costs will be calculated, how changes will be approved and how the completed work will be accepted.

Before Work Begins

A Price Without a Defined Scope Tells You Very Little

Two installation quotations may appear to cover the same service but actually include very different work.

One estimate may cover installation only. Another may include finishing elements, unloading, lifting, sealing, junction details, consumables and other operations.

Before comparing prices, it is therefore essential to establish exactly what is included and excluded from each proposal.

Contract Structure

What Should Be Defined in a Construction Contract?

The specific terms depend on the project, but the main organizational matters should preferably be settled before work starts on site.

01 / SUBJECT

What Will Be Performed?

The type of work, facility, work area and expected result should be defined clearly enough to avoid ambiguity.

02 / SCOPE

Quantities and Work Boundaries

Areas, quantities, connection details, components and the division of responsibilities between different contractors.

03 / MATERIALS

Who Supplies the Materials?

The parties should specify who purchases the principal materials, accessories and consumables.

04 / PRICE

How Is the Price Determined?

A fixed contract price, payment based on actual quantities or another calculation method agreed by the parties.

05 / TIMELINE

When Will Work Begin?

The start date depends not only on the calendar but also on site readiness, structures, material availability and access.

06 / PAYMENT

Payment Stages

Advance payments, interim payments, milestone payments or payment after confirmation of the agreed work quantities.

07 / VARIATIONS

Additional Work

New tasks and changes to the original scope should preferably be approved before they are performed.

08 / ACCEPTANCE

How Is Completion Confirmed?

The procedure for inspections, comments, documentation and confirmation of completed quantities.

09 / WARRANTY

Responsibility for the Result

Warranty matters depend on applicable law, the type of work and the terms of the specific contract.

Work Process

How to Organize a Project Before Starting On Site

The more accurate the initial information, the lower the risk of unexpected quantities and disputes about whether particular work was included in the original price.

01

Initial Information

Designs, dimensions, photographs, quantity schedules and technical requirements.

02

Estimation

Work quantities, materials and required operations are identified.

03

Proposal

A clear breakdown of the scope and associated costs is prepared.

04

Contract

The agreed terms of cooperation are documented.

05

Execution

Work proceeds according to the agreed scope and arrangements.

Materials

Who Purchases the Materials Is Part of the Agreement

For some projects, the client has already purchased the materials. For others, selection and supply arrangements are required. These two models should be distinguished clearly.

Client-Supplied Materials

Where the client provides materials, their actual availability and completeness should preferably be checked before installation.

  • Availability of principal materials;
  • Completeness of the supply;
  • Finishing and supplementary elements;
  • Fasteners and consumables;
  • Condition of materials after delivery;
  • Suitability for the intended work.

Supply Through PANEL MONTAGE

Where supply forms part of the agreed scope, materials can be coordinated with project estimation and subsequent installation.

  • Determining required quantities;
  • Material selection;
  • Preparing a complete supply package;
  • Coordinating deliveries;
  • Aligning deliveries with installation stages;
  • Working with a network of manufacturers and suppliers.
Changes

Additional Work Should Be Agreed Before It Is Performed

During construction, circumstances may arise that were not apparent when the original estimate was prepared, or the client may change the requirements.

For example, the area may change, new openings may be added, connection details may be revised, or dismantling, reinforcement, additional sealing or other work may become necessary.

Such variations should be distinguished from the original scope, with their costs and effects on the schedule agreed separately.

When Requirements Change

01Record the new circumstance or requirement.
02Define the additional scope.
03Calculate the cost.
04Assess the effect on the schedule.
05Obtain agreement between the parties.

Work That Will Be Concealed by Subsequent Operations Requires Early Attention

If a particular stage cannot be properly inspected after further construction work, the method of inspection and documentation should be established before the next operations begin. Where the law and project documentation require inspection of concealed works, the applicable formal documentation procedure must be followed.

Acceptance

Completed Work Should Be Assessed Against the Agreed Scope

Acceptance is not simply a matter of whether someone likes the result. It requires an understanding of what was agreed, what was actually completed and whether there are specific comments or defects.

Work Completed

The actual result is compared with the contract, estimate, design and other agreed documents.

Comments or Defects Identified

Comments should be specific, identifying the location, nature, extent and relevant element of the work.

Additional Work Identified

Originally agreed work should be distinguished from additional tasks introduced later.

A Dispute Arises

Start by reconstructing the documentary record: contract, appendices, correspondence, certificates, design documents and the actual condition of the facility.

Practical Risks

Common Causes of Disagreements

01

Unclear Scope

The parties interpret differently which operations were included in the quoted price.

02

Missing Materials

The principal materials are available, but accessories, fasteners or finishing elements are missing.

03

Site Not Ready

Materials and installers arrive, but the preceding construction stage has not been completed.

04

Requirements Change

After agreeing on the price, the client changes the building configuration or scope.

05

Undocumented Variations

Additional work is discussed verbally, resulting in a later disagreement about cost.

06

Unclear Acceptance Procedure

The parties have not defined how completed quantities will be confirmed or comments documented.

Quality and Warranty

Warranty Matters Must Be Considered for Each Specific Project

Responsibility for quality depends on the nature of the work, design solutions, contract terms, applicable legislation, building operation and the cause of the particular defect.

Why We Do Not Advertise a “Lifetime Warranty”

This website does not make unrealistic promises about outcomes. Terms applicable to a specific project should be established by the contract and the relevant legal requirements.

When a Construction Issue Becomes a Legal Dispute

Disputes involving contracts, payments, quality, construction documentation, land, commissioning or government requirements are legal matters. For such questions, you can visit a separate specialist legal resource.

Frequently Asked Questions

Construction Contracts and Work Procedures

Can I receive an estimate before signing a contract?

Yes. First, the project information, proposed scope of work and materials should be identified. The terms for the specific project can then be formalized in a contract.

What if the exact scope of work is not known in advance?

The parties should identify the known scope, the information requiring clarification and the method for calculating variations or actual quantities of completed work.

Can PANEL MONTAGE supply materials and arrange installation?

Depending on the project, a coordinated scope may include estimating, material selection, supply arrangements and agreed installation works.

What happens if additional work is required during installation?

Additional work should first be defined and assessed, then its cost, timing and effect on the original scope should be agreed.

How should defects or comments on completed work be recorded?

Each comment should identify the relevant location or element, describe the issue and specify the agreed requirement against which the completed work is being assessed.

Can I obtain legal assistance with a construction dispute?

PANEL MONTAGE is a construction resource. Legal disputes, contractual claims and regulatory matters should be considered separately with a qualified lawyer or advocate.
