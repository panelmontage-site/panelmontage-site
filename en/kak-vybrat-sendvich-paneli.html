How to Choose Sandwich Panels for Your Building

Sandwich panels should not be selected solely on price or thickness. First, you need to understand where the panel will be used: in a wall or roof, for what type of building, under which temperature conditions, on what supporting frame, and with which connection details.

Category: Sandwich Panels Author: PANEL MONTAGE Practical Guide
Read the Guide Request a Project Estimate

Choosing the right sandwich panels begins not with a manufacturer's catalogue, but with the characteristics of the building itself. A warehouse, production workshop, cold storage facility, hangar and commercial building each place different demands on the building envelope.

The panel itself is only one component of the system. The final result also depends on the steel frame, building geometry, fasteners, connections, openings, flashings, sealing and installation quality.

The Main Principle

First, define the building's purpose and operating conditions. Next, determine the wall and roof systems. Only then should the panel specifications, accessories and installation details be selected.

1. Start with the Building's Intended Use

The first question is what activities will take place inside the building. Its intended use affects requirements for the building envelope, indoor temperature, humidity, ventilation and internal surfaces.

For example, an ordinary unheated warehouse has different requirements from a production facility, a permanently heated building or a room where a specific temperature must be maintained.

WAREHOUSE

Storage

Consider operating conditions, stored products, heating, loading doors and traffic intensity.

INDUSTRIAL

Production Workshop

Internal processes, ventilation, equipment, openings and engineering utilities are important.

COLD STORAGE

Temperature-Controlled Space

The entire insulated envelope must be considered, including joints, doors, floors, ceilings and operating conditions.

COMMERCIAL

Business Facility

In addition to functionality, facade architecture and the building's appearance may be important.

If your project is still at the concept stage, it is useful to define its purpose and basic parameters through our project estimation page.

2. Do Not Confuse Wall Panels with Roof Panels

Walls and roofs operate under different conditions, so the panels used for each should be considered separately.

Wall Sandwich Panels

Wall panels form external or internal enclosures. When selecting them, consider panel layout, installation direction, opening positions, joints, corners, base connections and upper connections.

We discuss these issues separately on our wall sandwich panels page.

Roof Sandwich Panels

For roofing, particular attention should be paid to the support arrangement, installation direction, longitudinal and transverse connections, roof ridge, eaves, drainage and penetrations through the roof envelope.

For further information, visit our roof sandwich panels section.

A Common Practical Mistake

Ordering panels before the layout, openings and main connection details have been determined is risky. Material quantities and panel lengths must correspond to the building's actual geometry.

3. Choose Insulation According to the Application, Not Its Name

The panel's insulation core determines important properties of the building envelope. Different types of insulation cores are available on the market, so comparing panels solely by appearance is inappropriate.

Material selection should consider the following requirements together:

  • The building's intended use.
  • Required temperature conditions.
  • Requirements of the specific project.
  • Fire performance characteristics of the material.
  • Moisture exposure and operating conditions.
  • Required thermal insulation performance.
  • The wall or roof system design.
  • Manufacturer documentation for the specific panel.

Therefore, no single type of insulation core should automatically be considered the best choice for every project. The selected solution must meet the requirements of the particular building and its design.

4. Panel Thickness Alone Does Not Determine Performance

One of the most common questions is: "What panel thickness do I need?" Without information about the building, this question cannot be answered accurately.

At a minimum, the following factors should be considered:

  • Construction region.
  • Intended use of the space.
  • Indoor temperature requirements.
  • Whether the panel is used in a wall or roof.
  • Characteristics of the specific insulation material.
  • Overall building envelope design.
  • Project requirements.
Why We Do Not Give One Universal Thickness

The same thickness does not mean identical performance for different panels. Even similar buildings may require different solutions when their operating conditions differ.

5. Pay Attention to the Panel Surfaces

A sandwich panel does more than provide thermal insulation. Its metal facings are exposed to external and internal environmental conditions.

When choosing panels, consider:

  • Operating conditions of the facility.
  • Humidity.
  • Possible exposure to aggressive environments.
  • Requirements for internal surfaces.
  • Facade colour and architectural appearance.
  • Manufacturer specifications for the protective coating.

For buildings where appearance is an important architectural consideration, sandwich panels may be combined with separate facade solutions .

6. Check the Steel Frame Geometry Before Ordering

Even high-quality panels cannot compensate for problems with the supporting structure. If the actual frame geometry differs from the design, problems may become apparent during panel installation.

Before work begins, establish:

  • Whether the frame is ready for installation.
  • Whether support positions match the design.
  • Whether openings have been prepared.
  • How corners and connections will be completed.
  • Where engineering utilities will pass through.
  • Whether additional work will be required.

Steel frame considerations are covered separately in our steel structure installation section.

7. The Panel Is Not the Entire Building Envelope

When purchasing materials, clients often calculate only the square metres of panels. However, a completed building envelope includes considerably more components.

Depending on the design, the following may be required:

  • Fasteners.
  • Sealing materials.
  • Joint sealants.
  • Corner flashings.
  • Base connections.
  • Eaves flashings.
  • Ridge flashings.
  • Window and door opening details.
  • Additional trim and flashing components.
  • Details for engineering service penetrations.

Material selection should therefore be carried out with an understanding of the intended installation arrangement.

8. Installation Quality Is as Important as Panel Quality

A panel may meet its stated specifications, but installation mistakes can compromise the performance of the entire building envelope.

Particular attention is required at joints, connections, openings, roof interfaces and engineering service penetrations.

We have prepared a separate practical article covering common sandwich panel installation mistakes .

9. The Cheapest Panel Does Not Necessarily Mean the Cheapest Building

Comparing offers solely by price per square metre is insufficient. The final project cost depends on much more than the panels themselves.

Consider the following:

  • Completeness of the material supply.
  • Panel lengths and layout.
  • Flashings and additional components.
  • Fasteners and consumables.
  • Delivery.
  • Unloading and storage.
  • Lifting equipment.
  • Installation complexity.
  • Openings and connections.
  • Readiness of the supporting frame.

This is why our sandwich panel installation cost page focuses on project-specific information rather than a single universal price.

10. What to Compare in Commercial Quotations

When you receive several quotations, compare offers with an equivalent scope of supply. Otherwise, a lower price may simply mean that some necessary items have been excluded.

What to Compare Why It Matters
Panel type Wall and roof panels are designed for different applications.
Insulation core Its properties must match the building's requirements.
Thickness Thickness should be evaluated together with the specific panel's characteristics.
Metal facings and coatings These affect operating performance and surface durability.
Accessories Missing components change the actual cost of a complete package.
Delivery The destination region and shipment volume affect logistics.
Installation It is important to establish exactly which works are included.

Building materials can also be considered as a complete package in our materials and equipment section.

A Short Checklist Before Ordering

Before confirming an order, it is advisable to have answers to the following questions:

  • What type of building are the panels intended for?
  • What indoor operating conditions are required?
  • Are wall panels, roof panels or both needed?
  • Are design documents and a panel layout available?
  • Have the frame dimensions and geometry been checked?
  • Have the openings been defined?
  • Is the full list of accessories clear?
  • Have delivery and unloading been considered?
  • Who will carry out the installation?
  • Which works are included in the quoted price?
If Some Information Is Not Yet Available

You can still begin the estimation process. Provide the building's purpose, city, approximate dimensions and available documents. The missing information can then be identified.

Conclusion

A sandwich panel should be selected as part of a specific building, not as an isolated product. Building purpose, temperature conditions, wall and roof systems, supporting frame, openings, engineering systems and installation details all form one interconnected system.

The earlier these elements are considered together, the easier it becomes to compare materials, quantities, costs and the sequence of works.
