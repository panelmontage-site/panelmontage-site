Common Sandwich Panel Installation Mistakes

Even a high-quality sandwich panel does not guarantee a properly constructed building envelope. The result depends on steel frame geometry, material storage, fasteners, joints, connections, openings, flashings and the sequence of installation works.

Category: Installation Author: PANEL MONTAGE Practical Guide
Explore the Mistakes Request a Project Estimate

Most problems with a building envelope cannot be attributed to a single cause. Sandwich panels, the supporting frame, fasteners, sealing materials, flashings and installation work form one interconnected system.

Therefore, assessing installation quality solely by how straight the facade looks is insufficient. Some mistakes become apparent during installation, while others only emerge after the building has been put into operation.

The Main Principle

It is generally less costly to prevent a mistake before a wall or roof is closed than to correct it after the entire building envelope has been completed.

1. Installing Panels on an Unprepared Steel Frame

Mistake 01

Installation begins without checking the actual frame geometry

Sandwich panels are installed on an existing supporting structure. If support positions, vertical alignment, surface planes or opening dimensions differ from the intended design, these discrepancies affect the building envelope.

Possible Consequences

Difficulties joining panels, misalignment, additional adjustments and extra work.

What to Check

Frame readiness, support positions, geometry, openings and actual dimensions.

The supporting frame and the building envelope must be considered together. Read more about steel structure installation .

2. Improper Unloading and Storage of Panels

Mistake 02

Materials are damaged before installation even begins

Sandwich panels pass through several stages: transportation, unloading, temporary storage, movement around the construction site and lifting into installation position.

At every stage, it is important to follow the specific manufacturer's requirements for handling the material.

Possible Consequences

Damage to metal facings, deformed edges, joint alignment problems and reduced visual quality.

What to Check

Packaging condition, unloading method, storage location, material protection and panel handling procedures.

3. Installing Panels Without a Defined Layout

Mistake 03

Panel lengths and installation sequence are decided on site

Panel layout should ideally be coordinated with the building geometry, openings, corners, joints and architectural elements before installation begins.

Possible Consequences

Excessive cutting, material waste, inconvenient joints, delays and changes to the original design.

What to Check

Panel layout, lengths, installation direction, openings, corners and installation sequence.

This issue begins at the material selection stage. See our guide: How to Choose Sandwich Panels .

4. Incorrect Panel Fastening

Mistake 04

Fasteners are installed without considering the panel system and supporting structure

Fastener type, positioning and installation method must correspond to the specific panel system, supporting structure, design and manufacturer's recommendations.

Possible Consequences

Surface deformation, problems at fastening points or unreliable panel attachment.

What to Check

Fastener type, supporting structure, fastening point positions and technical documentation.

5. Poorly Executed Panel Joints

Mistake 05

Attention is focused on the panel surface rather than the connections between panels

A joint is one of the key components of the building envelope. Its performance depends on panel geometry, correct installation and the specified sealing system.

Possible Consequences

Loss of joint tightness, localized building envelope problems and deterioration in appearance.

What to Check

Joint cleanliness, geometry, correct panel engagement and specified sealing materials.

6. Treating Sealing as a Secondary Task

Mistake 06

Sealant is used as a universal solution for every connection detail

Sealing is part of a properly designed connection detail. It does not replace correct geometry, the specified connection, fastening or flashing components.

Possible Consequences

The underlying problem is temporarily concealed rather than corrected.

What to Check

Connection design, material compatibility, surface preparation and the intended purpose of the sealing product.

7. Leaving Openings Until Later

Mistake 07

Windows, doors and loading gates are not coordinated with the panel layout

Openings affect panel layout, cutting, framing, flashings and the sequence of installation work.

It is especially important to establish the positions of large loading gates and other elements connected to the supporting steel frame in advance.

Possible Consequences

Excessive cutting, complicated connections, additional materials and rework.

What to Check

Opening dimensions and positions, surrounding structural framing, trims and panel layout.

8. Underestimating Flashings and Accessories

Mistake 08

Panel quantities are calculated, but the finishing details are omitted

Corners, base connections, eaves, roof ridges, openings and other interfaces require appropriate construction details.

Flashings and accessories are not merely decorative additions. They form part of the completed building envelope.

Possible Consequences

Incomplete connections, installation delays, additional orders and differences between estimated and actual costs.

What to Check

The complete list of connection details and accessories before purchasing the main panel materials.

For this reason, it is useful to calculate not only the square metres of panels but also the entire required material package. Read more about factors affecting installation costs .

9. Treating Roof Panel Installation Like Wall Installation

Mistake 09

The specific requirements of the roof envelope are overlooked

Roofing requires particular attention to the support arrangement, installation direction, connections, roof ridge, eaves, drainage and penetrations.

Possible Consequences

Problems at joints and connections within the roof envelope.

What to Check

Roof design, supporting structure, joints, ridge, eaves and all service penetrations.

Roofing solutions are discussed separately on our roof sandwich panels page.

10. Planning Service Penetrations After Installation

Mistake 10

Ventilation and other building services are not coordinated with the envelope in advance

Air ducts, process utilities and other building services may need to pass through the wall or roof envelope.

If penetration locations become known only after installation is complete, the finished building envelope must be modified.

Possible Consequences

Additional cutting, new connection details, resealing and extra installation work.

What to Check

Locations of service penetrations and their relationship to the structures before walls and roofs are closed.

This is particularly relevant to ventilation and air-conditioning systems.

11. Incorrect Sequence of Construction Works

Mistake 11

Different types of work are performed without a coordinated sequence

Steel structures, sandwich panels, loading gates, building services and other elements may be installed by different contractors working on the same site.

If the work sequence has not been established in advance, completed elements may obstruct subsequent operations.

Possible Consequences

Downtime, repeated operations, damage to completed elements and longer project schedules.

What to Check

Work sequence, readiness of preceding stages and coordination points between different activities.

12. Leaving All Inspection Until the End

Mistake 12

Intermediate connection details are not inspected before being concealed

Some elements become difficult to inspect visually once the next stage of construction has been completed.

It is therefore useful to establish in advance which stages require intermediate inspection and documentation.

Possible Consequences

Disputes over the causes of defects, the need to open completed sections and difficulty determining responsibility.

What to Check

Inspection procedures, documentation of completed stages and acceptance arrangements applicable to the project.

Scope of work, materials, schedules, changes and acceptance procedures should ideally be agreed before work begins. See construction contracts and work procedures .

Where Problems Most Commonly Arise

Stage Typical Risk What to Check
Before installation Frame is not ready Geometry, supports and openings
Delivery Material damage Packaging, unloading and storage
Panel layout Excessive cutting Lengths, openings and installation direction
Fastening Incorrect attachment Fasteners, supporting structure and fastening layout
Joints Improper panel connections Geometry and sealing
Connections Incomplete details Flashings and sealing materials
Building services Cutting the completed envelope Penetrations before enclosure completion
Acceptance Concealed defects Intermediate inspections

Checklist Before Installation Begins

Before proceeding with the main installation work, it is useful to check not only whether the panels are available, but whether the entire site is ready.

01

Steel Frame

Geometry, support positions, openings and structural readiness.

02

Materials

Panels, fasteners, sealing materials and accessories.

03

Panel Layout

Installation sequence, lengths, joints and openings.

04

Equipment

Site access, unloading, lifting and material handling.

05

Building Services

Penetrations, ventilation and other utilities.

06

Quality Control

Inspection stages before details are concealed and final acceptance.

Conclusion

Many sandwich panel installation mistakes arise not from the panels themselves, but from insufficient coordination between different parts of the construction process.

The supporting frame, panels, accessories, openings, roofing, building services and work sequence should be considered as one interconnected system.

When the project's initial requirements are established in advance, it becomes easier to calculate materials, prepare the construction site, define the installation scope and reduce changes during construction.
