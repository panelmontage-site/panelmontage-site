Engineering System

Ventilation Is Calculated for the Specific Space and Its Intended Use

A warehouse, production workshop, store, service center or administrative space has different operating conditions.

Ventilation selection therefore does not begin with buying a fan, but with the initial data: floor area and room height, the purpose of individual zones, number of people, technological processes and other factors that affect air exchange.

For industrial facilities, it is especially important to consider the actual processes inside the building: heat, moisture, dust, odors or other contaminants that may be generated.

To Start the Calculation

02 Area, height and layout.
03 Number of people and operating schedule.
04 Description of technological processes.
05 Project city and site conditions.

Equipment and Materials

A Ventilation System Consists of Interconnected Components

The cost and characteristics of a system depend not on one piece of equipment, but on the complete configuration: duct routes, cross-sections, equipment placement, number of rooms and required functions.

Equipment should therefore be selected after the engineering requirements have been defined, rather than the other way around.

Industrial Facilities

Workshop Ventilation Starts with Understanding the Production Process

For an industrial space, knowing only its floor area is not enough. It is necessary to understand what happens inside the building: where the work areas are located, what equipment operates there and where heat, moisture, dust or other emissions may be generated.

This information makes it possible to distinguish general ventilation requirements from tasks where localized air extraction may be required directly at the source.

03

04

Ventilation and Air Conditioning Are Not the Same

An air-conditioning system primarily controls indoor air temperature, while a ventilation system organizes air supply, removal and movement. These tasks should therefore be considered separately for a facility and then coordinated with each other.

Frequently Asked Questions

Ventilation Systems for Facilities

Can ventilation be calculated without a completed project?

Preliminary planning can begin using floor plans, dimensions, room purposes and a description of the facility. Additional initial data may be required during the process.

What is the difference between supply and exhaust ventilation?

A supply system introduces air into the building, while an exhaust system removes air. At many facilities, these functions are considered together.

Can equipment supply and installation be ordered together?

Yes. Calculation, equipment selection, system completion, supply and installation can be considered as one integrated scope of work.

Does an industrial workshop require a separate ventilation solution?

The solution depends on technological processes, individual spaces, work areas and factors generated during production. Initial data for the specific workshop must therefore be reviewed first.

Does air conditioning replace ventilation?

No. These are different engineering functions. Air conditioning is primarily associated with indoor temperature parameters, while ventilation provides organized air exchange.

What determines the cost of a ventilation system?

The cost depends on the purpose and dimensions of the spaces, required system configuration, equipment, length and configuration of ductwork, installation height, automation, condition of the facility and project region.

Do you work outside Almaty?

Yes. Projects are considered in Almaty, Astana and other regions of Kazakhstan, taking into account the scope of work and logistics.
