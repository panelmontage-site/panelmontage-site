Where to Start

Cost Estimation Is Possible at Different Project Stages

The information available to each client is different. Some already have complete designs and specifications, while others only know the approximate dimensions and intended purpose of the building. The first step is therefore to determine the appropriate level of detail for the estimate.

01

You Have a Completed Design

Submit the design documents, drawings, specifications and available bills of quantities. This makes it possible to proceed with a more specific cost calculation.

02

You Have Dimensions and a Technical Brief

If the complete design is not yet available, we can review the existing building parameters and determine whether a preliminary estimate can be prepared.

03

You Only Have a Building Idea

The process can begin by clarifying the initial requirements, developing a preliminary concept or identifying the design work needed before a meaningful cost estimate can be prepared.

Level of Detail

A Preliminary Estimate and a Detailed Cost Estimate Are Not the Same

We distinguish between these approaches so clients understand which information has been used to calculate the costs.

Preliminary Cost Estimate

Used at an early project stage when some design decisions have not yet been finalized.

  • Helps establish an approximate project budget.
  • May be based on general building parameters.
  • Includes assumptions where information is incomplete.
  • Is refined as the design develops.

More Detailed Cost Estimate

Possible when sufficient design and technical information is available.

  • Uses more accurate quantities.
  • Allows a more detailed breakdown of work categories.
  • Accounts for specifications and selected technical solutions.
  • Reduces the number of undefined cost items.
Why Estimate Costs in Advance?

A Cost Estimate Is More Than a Final Number

A structured estimate helps identify which parts of the project account for the budget and where technical or commercial decisions are needed.

Budget Planning

Provides a basis for planning the project's financing requirements.

Comparing Alternatives

Helps assess how materials and specific technical solutions affect the overall cost.

Scope Control

Makes it easier to understand which materials and works are included in the estimate.

Procurement Planning

Once quantities are established, material and supplier selection can begin.

Frequently Asked Questions

Construction Cost Estimation

Can construction costs be estimated without a completed design?

In some cases, a preliminary estimate can be prepared using the available building parameters. Its accuracy depends on the completeness of the information. If the information is insufficient, preliminary concept development or further design work may be necessary.

Can I submit an existing construction estimate?

Yes. You can submit an existing estimate together with the design and other documents to help clarify the cost structure and the required scope of analysis.

Can I request an estimate for installation work only?

Yes. PANEL MONTAGE offers a separate service for estimating installation work costs.

Can I order materials after receiving the estimate?

Yes. Once the required quantities have been determined, material and equipment selection and supply arrangements can be considered.

Can PANEL MONTAGE carry out the construction or installation work after preparing the estimate?

Yes, provided the required work falls within PANEL MONTAGE's areas of activity. The scope, schedule, execution conditions and contractual terms are discussed separately.

Do you work with projects in different regions of Kazakhstan?

Yes. Projects in Almaty, Astana, Shymkent, Karaganda, Atyrau, Aktau and other regions of Kazakhstan are considered.
